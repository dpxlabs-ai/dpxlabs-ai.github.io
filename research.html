01One-Dimensional Turbulence

One line through a turbulent world.

One-Dimensional Turbulence follows a single line of sight through a turbulent flow and resolves every scale along it, down to where mixing, heat release and chemistry actually happen. Three-dimensional eddies are represented by stochastic rearrangements of that line. The result keeps the physics that matter at a small fraction of a full 3D simulation's cost.

Why it matters for AI. A compact representation that still respects the physics is exactly what a learning system needs: cheaper training data, a sharper inductive bias, and predictions that can be checked against the equations.

ODT was introduced by Alan Kerstein at Sandia National Laboratories. Our team has developed and applied ODT-based methods for turbulent reacting flows. Selected papers

02Areas

Four questions we are working on.

  • Neural Operators

    Learning mappings between functions, not between fixed grids of numbers, so that one model generalizes across geometries and conditions.

  • Physics-Informed Learning

    Conservation laws as hard constraints, not just penalty terms. A model that can break a mass or energy balance can't be trusted, however small its error.

  • Generative Physical Models

    Sampling plausible physical states, not only point predictions. Generative models have to earn their place against simpler baselines.

  • Scientific Computing

    Reduced-order models, solvers, verified data generation and high-performance computing: the foundation every learned model stands on.

  1. (1)

    Physics first. A differentiable physics model sits underneath. Learning corrects it rather than replacing it.

  2. (2)

    Every prediction carries a certificate. A physics residual, a calibrated interval and an out-of-distribution flag. Anything outside the trusted region goes to a full solver.

  3. (3)

    Every fallback teaches the model. Solver results become verified training data.

04Principles

Rules we work by.

Validate against physics.
Mass and energy must close. Plausible-looking doesn't count.
Quantify uncertainty.
A prediction without calibrated error bars is unfinished.
Simple baselines first.
A complex model ships only when it beats the simple ones.
Honest about stage.
Results are published when they exist, not before.
